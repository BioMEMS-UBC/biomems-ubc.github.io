Inkjet printing provides a versatile approach for dispensing living cells in precisely controlled droplets, with applications ranging from biofabrication to high-throughput single-cell analysis. However, reliable cell printing requires an understanding of the complex interactions between suspended cells, fluid flow, and droplet formation, as well as methods for accurately controlling and verifying cell deposition.

Our early research investigated the fluid mechanics of piezoelectric inkjet printing using high-speed imaging and micro-particle image velocimetry (micro-PIV). We characterized transient, oscillatory flow fields within tapered glass nozzles and examined how individual cells respond during droplet ejection. These studies revealed distinct cell behaviours, including cell transport, ejection, and reflection within the nozzle. By modifying the physical properties of cell suspensions, we demonstrated approaches to reducing undesirable cell motion and improving dispensing reliability.

Building on this foundation, we developed Isolatrix, an automated single-cell printing platform that integrates inkjet dispensing with real-time optical feedback and machine learning. Rather than relying solely on predictions of cell occupancy before dispensing, the system analyzes dispensing events to identify successful single-cell deposition. This approach achieved 98.7% classification accuracy and dispensing times as low as 0.52 seconds per cell, providing up to a 9.69-fold improvement in isolation speed compared with predictive workflows. We demonstrated its application to single-cell whole-genome sequencing, including the analysis of chromosomal copy number alterations.

We subsequently extended this technology to single-cell proteomics through the SPIN workflow, which combines image-guided cell dispensing with nanowell-based sample preparation and precise environmental control. Dew-point regulation minimizes evaporation during processing, while low-contact dispensing reduces sample loss. Compared with a commercial dispensing system, this approach improved protein and peptide recovery and enabled reproducible proteomic profiling of individual cells.

Together, these developments connect fundamental inkjet fluid mechanics with automated, intelligent cell handling and downstream molecular analysis. Our work advances the precision and scalability of single-cell sample preparation, supporting studies of cellular heterogeneity across genomic and proteomic applications.

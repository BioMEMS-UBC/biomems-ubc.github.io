Silicon photonic biosensors

Biosensors: Applications and Future Directions

Exploring how more accessible molecular measurements could support biological research.

  • Overview
  • Research and technology
  • Applications and future directions

Expanding access to molecular measurements

Many biological processes involve molecular changes that are difficult to capture through occasional laboratory measurements. Our research explores how silicon photonic biosensors could provide more accessible, quantitative measurements of multiple biomarkers, opening new opportunities to investigate biological processes over time.

While our current work focuses on developing and validating the underlying sensing technologies, we are interested in several areas where these capabilities could eventually be useful.

Women’s health and hormonal dynamics

Hormonal changes occur across menstrual cycles, between individuals, and during transitions such as perimenopause. However, detailed longitudinal measurements remain difficult to obtain, limiting our understanding of how these changes relate to health and physiological function.

Our demonstration of multiplexed FSH and PdG detection provides an initial foundation for developing more comprehensive hormone measurement capabilities. Future research could investigate individual hormonal trajectories, relationships between hormonal variability and symptoms, and potential implications for treatment response.

Neurological injury and recovery

Molecular biomarkers may provide information about neurological injury and recovery that complements clinical assessments and imaging.

We are developing silicon photonic assays for neurofilament light chain (NfL), a biomarker associated with neuroaxonal injury. Longer-term applications could include studying biomarker changes following traumatic brain injury, cardiac arrest, and other neurological conditions.

Therapeutic development and precision medicine

Physiological conditions can change over time and may influence responses to treatment. Better access to molecular measurements could help researchers investigate these relationships.

One emerging area of interest is whether naturally occurring reproductive hormone dynamics influence the performance of emerging therapies, including lipid nanoparticle-based drug delivery. This remains an open scientific question that would benefit from interdisciplinary investigation.

Infectious disease and health security

The sensing platform can potentially be adapted to different molecular targets through changes in the recognition chemistry.

Our research has included assays for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein as a model target. Future applications could include infectious disease testing, health security, and distributed molecular measurements in settings where access to conventional laboratory infrastructure is limited.

Biological and environmental systems

Beyond clinical diagnostics, multiplexed molecular sensing could provide new ways to study biological and environmental systems.

Potential applications include monitoring molecular signaling in engineered tissues and organ-on-chip models, as well as detecting selected contaminants or biological targets in environmental samples.

These directions remain exploratory and would require application-specific assay development and validation.

Looking ahead

Our current research focuses on advancing the underlying sensing technologies and establishing the capabilities needed for future applications. We are interested in scientific exchanges with researchers and organizations working on related measurement challenges, which may help inform future research directions.

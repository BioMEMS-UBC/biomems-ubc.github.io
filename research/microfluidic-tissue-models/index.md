The cellular microenvironment plays a central role in regulating cell behaviour, tissue function, and responses to therapeutic interventions. Conventional two-dimensional cell cultures provide limited control over many features of this environment, including three-dimensional tissue organization, extracellular matrix interactions, fluid transport, and spatial and temporal variations in oxygen availability. Our research develops microfluidic and organ-on-chip technologies that incorporate these features into experimentally controlled in vitro models.

## Microfluidic Models of Tumour Hypoxia

Oxygen availability is an important feature of the tumour microenvironment, influencing cancer cell behaviour and responses to treatment. In solid tumours, irregular vascular networks and fluctuating blood flow can produce both chronic hypoxia and transient cycles of hypoxia and reoxygenation.

We have developed microfluidic platforms that control oxygen conditions over space and time, enabling the study of three-dimensional tumour spheroids under physiologically relevant oxygen profiles. By integrating controlled oxygen delivery with live-cell microscopy, these systems allow us to investigate changes in tumour spheroid morphology, cell behaviour, and responses to anticancer treatments. This work provides tools for examining biological responses to environmental conditions that are difficult to reproduce using conventional cell culture systems.

## Three-Dimensional Cell Culture and Engineered Tumour Models

Three-dimensional cell culture provides opportunities to reproduce aspects of tissue architecture and cell-cell and cell-matrix interactions that are absent in conventional monolayer cultures. We develop microfluidic approaches for generating and maintaining cell-laden hydrogel structures, incorporating extracellular matrix components such as collagen and basement membrane-derived materials.

These engineered microenvironments support investigations of cellular organization, tissue growth, and responses to environmental conditions and therapeutic compounds. We have also developed microfluidic tumour models designed to improve experimental access to three-dimensional cultures, facilitating sample manipulation, observation, and downstream analysis.

## Organ-on-Chip Platforms and Engineered Tissue Interfaces

Many physiological processes depend on interactions between neighbouring tissues separated by thin extracellular matrix interfaces. Organ-on-chip technologies offer a means of recreating selected structural and functional features of these interfaces while controlling fluid transport and cellular organization.

Our research includes microfluidic platforms incorporating ultrathin extracellular matrix-derived membranes, with tunable physical and transport properties, to support cell culture and studies of tissue interfaces. Related work on airway and other microengineered tissue models explores how device architecture, biomaterial properties, and cell culture conditions can be integrated to create experimentally accessible representations of tissue environments.

Together, these research directions combine microfluidic engineering, biomaterials, and tissue culture to investigate how cellular microenvironments influence biological behaviour and responses to therapeutic interventions.

Our work in engineering education explores how educational experiences, institutional practices, and social factors influence participation, learning, and professional development in engineering. This research encompasses two complementary directions: understanding inclusion and participation in engineering pathways, and developing educational approaches that prepare engineers for interdisciplinary and translational practice.

## Engineering identity, inclusion, and STEM outreach

In collaboration with colleagues in engineering education and the social sciences, we investigate factors that influence young people's engagement with engineering, particularly among groups historically underrepresented in the profession. Our studies examine engineering and STEM identity, the influence of family backgrounds and role models, and the extent to which outreach programs reach and benefit different student populations.

This work combines systematic literature reviews with empirical studies using validated survey instruments and quantitative analyses of student experiences. By examining both changes in engineering identity and differences in participants' prior exposure to STEM, we aim to better understand the opportunities and limitations of outreach programs and inform more inclusive approaches to engineering engagement.

## Interdisciplinary and translational engineering education

A complementary area of our work examines educational models that prepare engineering students to address complex biomedical challenges. This includes research on clinically immersive, needs-driven graduate education, where students integrate engineering design with clinical observation, problem identification, interdisciplinary collaboration, and translational considerations.

Through the study of educational program development and outcomes, we contribute to understanding how engineering curricula can cultivate technical, collaborative, and decision-making skills relevant to biomedical innovation.

Together, these activities reflect an interest in both who participates in engineering and how engineers are educated to apply their expertise in diverse professional and societal contexts.

## Collaborative Research: Cultivating Inclusion in Engineering Lab (CIEL)

We are pleased to contribute to the Cultivating Inclusion in Engineering Lab (CIEL) at UBC, an interdisciplinary research team bringing together expertise in engineering, education, and the social sciences to better understand and address barriers to participation in engineering. Working alongside colleagues who lead this research, we have contributed to studies examining STEM outreach, engineering identity, and the influence of family background and prior experiences on young people's engagement with engineering. This collaboration has provided a valuable opportunity to contribute to evidence-based approaches for broadening participation in engineering, while learning from colleagues with complementary expertise in educational research.

Spinal cord injury disrupts neural pathways and creates a complex tissue environment that limits axonal regeneration. Injectable biomaterials offer a potential approach to filling injury cavities and providing structural and biochemical support for neural growth. However, their successful application requires careful control of material properties, internal architecture, and compatibility with the surrounding neural tissue.

Our research focuses on the development and characterization of engineered hydrogels for neural tissue repair, particularly gelatin methacrylate (GelMA), a photocrosslinkable biomaterial with tunable mechanical properties. We investigate how hydrogel formulation, crosslinking conditions, and material stiffness influence neurite extension in three-dimensional cultures. Using custom microscale devices containing primary neural explants, we develop experimental models to study neural growth within defined biomaterial environments. Quantitative image analysis allows us to compare neurite outgrowth across different material formulations and identify conditions that better support neural extension.

A complementary area of research addresses the practical challenges of delivering and stabilizing these materials within injured tissue. We characterize the optical properties of hydrogels and use light-transport simulations to investigate the feasibility of photocrosslinking within spinal cord lesions. We also study hydrogels containing magnetically alignable microstructures, which are being developed to provide directional guidance for regenerating neural processes. Our work includes evaluating the stability of these structured materials under MRI exposure, an important consideration for their potential clinical use.

Together, these studies connect biomaterial design, microscale neural tissue models, and quantitative physical characterization. As part of the interdisciplinary Mend the Gap initiative, our research contributes to understanding how engineered material environments can support neural regeneration and inform the development of biomaterial-based approaches to spinal cord injury repair.

## Mend the Gap Collaboration

Our research is part of Mend the Gap, a large international, interdisciplinary initiative led by the University of British Columbia and supported by the Government of Canada's New Frontiers in Research Fund. The initiative brings together researchers in biomaterials, neuroscience, engineering, rehabilitation, and clinical medicine, alongside partners from the spinal cord injury community, to develop new approaches for repairing the injured spinal cord. Our group contributes expertise in microengineered tissue models, hydrogel characterization, and quantitative analysis of neural growth, helping to evaluate and refine biomaterials designed to support neural regeneration. This work forms part of a broader effort integrating injectable biomaterials, therapeutic interventions, neural stimulation, and image-guided delivery to advance spinal cord injury repair.
